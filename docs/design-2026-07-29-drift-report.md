## Problem Statement

The June design doc aimed PM OS at the PM individual contributor: a guided
context builder (`/pm-brain`) that makes AI outputs good enough for strategy,
discovery, and prioritization work. That framing is now the constraint rather
than the plan.

Two things forced the re-frame this session. First, the founder's own research
(`research/market-research.md`, `research/strategy-eval.md`) established that the
PM IC lane is occupied at every price point by founders with better distribution:
free (Huryn, MIT), $49 one-time (Gupta), $15-29/mo (ChatPRD), $39/seat (mySecond).
Second, the Whitespace comparison exposed *why* that lane caps out: the PM IC has
no P&L. Whitespace sells to owners and managing directors who own the number, and
every other part of their model hangs off that fact.

The reframed problem: **a Head of Product cannot tell, week to week, whether their
team is building what the strategy says they should be building.** Documented
intent lives in Notion and goes stale in three weeks. Actual behavior lives in
Linear/Jira, Slack threads, and customer calls. Nobody reconciles the two, because
reconciling them requires reading everything, which no human has time to do. When
the CEO asks in a QBR why the team shipped X instead of Y, the answer gets
reconstructed from memory and Slack search.

## Demand Evidence

Current tier: **founder conviction (Tier C)**, unchanged since June. No paying
user, no named current user, no behavioral demand. This is the fifth week the
design doc has carried "no current named PM" as its top open question. The
approach chosen below is chosen partly because it cannot be executed without
closing that gap.

Indirect evidence that the *reframed* problem is real:
- YC RFS (Diana Hu, verified at ycombinator.com/rfs, Summer 2026): "the system
  monitors what's happening, compares it to what should be happening, and adjusts"
  and "there's no product that connects all this context into a single intelligence
  layer that can reason across it, flag when engineering is building the wrong
  thing." That is the drift loop, named by YC as an unmet need.
- Whitespace's own use-case list includes "Weekly exec brief: produce a report of
  sales, margin, stock and cash, highlighting key topics that need a decision" and
  "What-if scenarios" — evidence that once a vertical brain exists, the buyer wants
  exactly this class of artifact.
- Granola's repositioning from notetaker to "enterprise AI context" at a $1.5B
  valuation (Mar 2026) confirms the input side is valuable and contested.

None of this is demand for *this* product from *a named person*. The Assignment
fixes that or kills the direction.

## Status Quo

What a Head of Product does today about roadmap drift:
- Keeps a strategy/bets doc in Notion or Confluence. It is accurate the week it is
  written and stale within a month.
- Reads Linear/Jira dashboards that report velocity and cycle time — activity, not
  intent. The dashboards cannot say "this sprint contradicts bet #2."
- Runs a Monday leadership sync where drift surfaces anecdotally, if someone
  happens to remember.
- Reconstructs the "why did we build X" narrative from memory plus Slack search
  when asked in a QBR or board prep.
- Hand-writes the monthly exec update by opening six tabs.

**Cost of the status quo is unquantified.** This is carried forward from the June
doc as an open question and is now part of the Assignment: the number to get is
hours spent on exec-update assembly plus one named instance of a bet the team
drifted off without noticing.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Buyer, ranked by the founder this session:**
1. **Head of Product / CPO** (primary) — team-level budget, $500-2k/mo, sells on
   whether the team is building the right thing.
2. **Founder / solo operator acting as own PM** (secondary) — has budget, no PM
   org, no procurement, fastest close.
3. **Fractional PM / consultant** (tertiary) — pays for leverage per client;
   doubles as the founder's consulting flywheel.

**Explicitly eliminated: the PM individual contributor seat.** This is the single
biggest change from the June doc. It removes PM OS from the $0-$39 knife-fight
against ChatPRD, mySecond, Gupta, and Huryn, and it means the guided-interview
onboarding is no longer the product.

**Wedge, ranked by the founder this session:**
1. **Weekly strategy drift report** (primary) — what the team decided and shipped
   this week versus the documented bets. Flags what drifted and which decision was
   contradicted by a customer call.
2. **Exec readout / QBR production** (secondary).
3. **Prioritization / tradeoff defense** (tertiary) — when eng capacity gets cut
   20%, what gets dropped and what that costs.

Note that #1 and #2 are the same artifact at two cadences. The drift report *is*
what the Head of Product owes their CEO, which is why it forwards itself upward.
That shareability is not a growth tactic bolted on; it is a property of choosing
this wedge.

**Systems it reads (the ERP equivalent):** Linear/Jira for shipped,
Notion/Confluence for documented intent, Gong/Granola/Fireflies transcripts for
what customers said, Slack for what got decided in a thread. PM OS replaces none
of them, exactly as Whitespace does not replace the ERP.

## The Whitespace Structural Map

The reference model, decomposed and mapped. This table is the design's spine.

| Layer | Whitespace | PM OS equivalent | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wedge | Inventory planning, weekly, denominated in cash | Weekly strategy drift report | chosen this session |
| System of record | Sits on top of the ERP, never replaces it | Linear/Jira + Notion + transcripts + Slack | no migration required |
| The brain | "A living model of your business" — never sold as memory | gbrain-class substrate, provenance-tagged | already decided; must stop being the pitch |
| Loop | Human review routes exceptions; every correction trains the model | Head of Product marks "not drift, we changed that bet" → provenance-tagged decision written back | collapses old Phase 4 into Phase 1 |
| Expansion | Inventory → sales/CS → AP/AR → exec brief → what-if | Drift → exec readout → tradeoff modeling → ramp/handoff | earned by agent #2, not claimed |
| Delivery | Book a call, 2-4 weeks live, done-with-you install | Same; the install is the consulting motion | legitimizes capped Path D |
| Pricing | No published price, usage/workflow based, "pays for itself in 3 months" | Payback as the anchor, not seat count | replaces $29-39/mo seat pricing |

Four of the seven layers already existed as decisions in the June doc. The two
that changed are the wedge/buyer pair and the delivery/pricing pair. The one that
was inverted is the loop: it moves from Phase 4 to a side effect of Phase 1.

## Constraints

- Founder has no audience yet; the creator-channel GTM the PM IC lane requires is
  a losing race (established in `research/strategy-eval.md` §2a). Top-down sales
  to a smaller number of buyers is the escape.
- `vivekally/PM-OS-with-Second-Brain` is a public repo. Personal workspace links
  and identifiers must never appear in it. The exact strings to scan for live in
  that repo's local-only `CLAUDE.md`, deliberately not reproduced here.
- Substrate decision stands: adopt gbrain-class self-hostable memory, do not build
  memory infrastructure. Memory infra is commoditizing (Mem0/AWS, Supermemory,
  Zep, Letta, plus gbrain MIT).
- Option A constraints #1 and #2 from the June doc (own the data store, per-object
  tenant/owner/scope field) are do-now-anyway. Multi-tenant retrofits are the
  classic painful rewrite even inside the PM product once teams are involved.
- Design system is Workbench B, dark (`DESIGN.md`): `#070A11` background, `#3DDC84`
  accent, Inter UI, JetBrains Mono, provenance colors documented/verbal/hunch/industry.
- Confidentiality: reading a company's Linear + Slack + customer calls is a higher
  trust bar than reading one PM's notes. Encryption at rest and an audit log of
  context reads/writes (Option A #4 and #5) move from "deferrable legal bundle" to
  "needed for the second design partner."

## Premises

All seven were put to the founder and all seven were accepted without revision.

1. **The buyer change is the product change, not a pricing change.** An IC wants
   faster PRDs. A Head of Product wants to know their team is building the right
   thing. Only the second is worth $500-2k/mo, and only the second needs a brain.
2. **Never sell the brain.** Whitespace sells reorder quantities and £96k of freed
   cash, never "memory." "Provenance-tagged markdown brain" is architecture, not
   pitch. Selling memory invites comparison to a free MIT repo and to Claude
   Projects, which is the trap `strategy-eval.md` §2b identified.
3. **The wedge reads from systems automatically; the guided interview is not the
   wedge.** Drift is documented intent versus actual behavior, and actual behavior
   only exists in Linear/Jira/Slack/transcripts. An interview cannot produce it,
   and interview output goes stale — mySecond ships a "context health" nag for
   exactly this reason.
4. **Review of the drift report is the self-evolving brain.** When the Head of
   Product marks "not drift, we consciously changed that bet," a provenance-tagged
   decision is captured for free. Phase 4 stops being a phase.
5. **Done-with-you delivery is a feature.** Book a call, 2-4 weeks live, no
   published price, payback as the anchor. The install is the consulting, and it
   funds the build.
6. **"AI OS" is earned by the second agent, not claimed by the first.** Whitespace
   says "better inventory planning is just the start" while listing twelve use
   cases, but has one wedge live in production. PM OS does not launch as an OS.
7. **T1 as written now tests the wrong thing.** The falsification test runs
   strategy memo / spec / prioritization through hand-paste versus structured
   context — all IC tasks, for the buyer just eliminated. The real test is
   `strategy-eval.md` kill criterion #6 promoted to primary: hand-build one drift
   report from one Head of Product's real Linear + Notion + three transcripts, and
   see whether they forward it to their CEO.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Concierge drift report (minimal viable)

Ship no product. Get read access to three Heads of Product's Linear/Jira, roadmap
docs, and last three call transcripts. Hand-build the weekly drift report using
Claude Code and the existing `/pm-brain` skill as *internal* tooling, not a
user-facing product. Email it Monday morning. Charge partner #1 $500/mo in week two.

- Effort: **S** (human: ~2 days per report / CC: ~2 hours per report)
- Risk: **Low**
- Reuses: `/pm-brain` as internal tooling, the provenance model from the June doc,
  Workbench B tokens for the report's visual form.
- Pros: tests the only kill criterion that matters now (did they forward it?);
  produces revenue and a named buyer inside three weeks, closing the gap the doc
  has carried since 2026-06-22; teaches what drift actually looks like before any
  of it is encoded, which is premise 4 executed by hand.
- Cons: does not scale; there is no product; it reads as consulting; no venture
  story until it is automated.

### Approach B: Drift agent on auto-ingest (ideal architecture, Whitespace-shaped)

Build the machine. MCP connectors for Linear/Jira, Notion, and transcript export
feed a gbrain-class substrate carrying the per-object scope field. A weekly drift
agent produces the report. A review surface captures every correction as a
provenance-tagged decision written back to the brain. Sell done-with-you: 2-4
week install, usage and workflow pricing, payback as the anchor.

- Effort: **L** (human: ~3 months / CC: ~3 weeks)
- Risk: **Med**
- Reuses: gbrain substrate (decision already made and installed), Option A
  constraints #1-#2, DESIGN.md provenance colors, existing deploy pipeline.
- Pros: the Whitespace shape end to end; retention is mechanical rather than
  habit-dependent; agent #2 (the exec brief) is nearly free once drift ships,
  which is how the OS claim gets earned per premise 6; matches the YC RFS loop
  language the market is currently rewarding.
- Cons: connector glue work; a cold start per customer until their sources connect;
  three to six months to first revenue if built before design partners exist.

### Approach C: Trojan exec brief (creative / lateral)

Lead not with drift but with the artifact the Head of Product already owes upward:
the monthly exec readout. Free, no login, no integration — upload a Jira export, a
roadmap doc, and three transcripts, receive a publishable readout with drift
findings embedded as "three things to flag." Paid tier runs it weekly and remembers.

- Effort: **M** (human: ~3 weeks / CC: ~4 days)
- Risk: **Med**
- Reuses: `pm-os-landing`, Workbench B design system, the "free shareable artifact"
  GTM the June Distribution Plan asked for and never specified.
- Pros: answers the "what if the user did nothing at all" test with zero setup; the
  output is the advertisement; one object serves as top-of-funnel, brain-seeding
  intake, and wedge demo simultaneously.
- Cons: a one-shot upload does not compound, so it fixes activation and not
  retention; risks becoming one more free PM tool that caps willingness to pay,
  which is the exact failure `market-research.md` §1a warns about.

## Recommended Approach

**A now, converging into B. C waits until B's engine exists.**

Chosen by the founder this session. The reasoning: the one unfixed problem in the
design doc since 2026-06-22 is "no current named PM," and A is the only approach
that physically cannot be executed without one. B can be built for months without
ever meeting a buyer, which is how the June plan quietly stalled. C can ship
without a buyer too, and would cap price on the way.

Sequencing and trigger conditions:

1. **Now through week 4 — A.** Three Heads of Product, hand-built weekly drift
   reports, partner #1 paying $500/mo. Instrument which context fed which finding
   from the first report (T3 from TODOS.md, unchanged and now load-bearing: it is
   what makes premise 4 measurable).
2. **Trigger for B:** partner #2 asks "can this run itself?" or the founder is
   spending more than one day per report per partner. Build order inside B is
   ingest first (transcripts, then Linear/Jira), then the drift agent, then the
   review surface. The review surface is not optional polish; it is the training
   loop.
3. **Trigger for C:** B's drift engine runs unattended for one partner. Then C is
   a real demo of a real product rather than a standalone free tool, and the
   capping risk mostly evaporates.
4. **Path D (consulting) stays capped** at roughly 20% of the week, and every
   engagement must dogfood the product. Under premise 5 the install *is* the
   consulting, so A and D stop competing for hours.

What this does to the old phase plan: Phase 1's guided interview demotes from
product to internal tool. Phase 2's skill library is deferred indefinitely (it is
commodity and it was never the moat). Phase 3's web UI becomes the review surface
inside B, arriving earlier than planned but scoped much smaller. Phase 4's
self-evolving brain stops being a phase and becomes the review loop.

## Open Questions

1. **Cost of the status quo is still unquantified.** Carried from June. Migraine
   or hangnail? The Assignment gets the number.
2. **Does the drift report survive being wrong?** Whitespace calibrates before
   going live and routes exceptions to humans. A drift report that flags three
   things and is wrong about two destroys trust faster than a stale Notion doc. The
   confidence and provenance display is a trust design problem, not a model problem.
3. **Cold start per customer.** How many weeks of Linear history plus how many
   transcripts before the first report is worth forwarding? A gives the number
   cheaply; B's install promise (2-4 weeks) depends on it.
4. **Transcript access is the weakest link.** Kill criterion #4 from
   `strategy-eval.md` still stands: will a Head of Product actually connect Gong or
   Granola exports? Ask on the first call, not the fifth.
5. **The PM-to-lawyer transferable core is still unnamed.** Five weeks unchanged.
   Worth noting that under this re-frame a candidate finally exists: the
   *documented-intent-versus-actual-behavior reconciliation loop* transfers to
   legal as matter-intent versus filed-work reconciliation. That is a hypothesis,
   not a decision.
6. **Trust and confidentiality bar rises with the buyer.** Reading a company's
   Linear, Slack, and customer calls is a different security conversation than
   reading one PM's notes. Option A #4-#5 (encryption at rest, audit log) likely
   need to land before design partner #2, not at a legal pivot.

## Success Criteria

- **Week 3:** one Head of Product has granted read access, received one hand-built
  drift report, and **forwarded it to someone senior to them.** The forward is the
  signal, not the compliment.
- **Week 4:** partner #1 paying $500/mo or formally committed. One quantified
  status-quo cost on record (hours per exec update, plus one named drifted bet).
- **Week 8:** three partners receiving weekly reports. Observable compounding:
  report #4 is better than report #1 because prior corrections are in the brain.
  Traceable via the T3 context-to-output instrumentation.
- **B's trigger met honestly:** a partner asks for automation, rather than the
  founder deciding it is time to build.
- **Kill signal:** three Heads of Product read the report, say it is interesting,
  and none forward it. That is kill criterion #6 firing. The wedge is not the
  wedge, and the drift thesis stops.

## Distribution Plan

- **A:** no distribution. Email, one buyer at a time, founder-led. Sourcing is the
  founder's Toronto product network, WillHire-era contacts, and PM leads reachable
  through the fractional/consulting motion. Whitespace's entire funnel is a
  30-minute Cal.com booking; A does not need better than that.
- **B:** done-with-you install, 2-4 weeks, no self-serve signup, no published
  price. Existing deploy pipeline covers the review surface.
- **C (later):** the free exec brief is the top-of-funnel artifact, and its output
  is the advertisement because it gets forwarded upward with the product's name on it.
- Do not run more than one GTM motion at once. This was true in June and is more
  true now that the buyer count per month is small and each one is high-touch.

## Dependencies

- **One named Head of Product with read access.** Hard blocker on everything. No
  substitute, no workaround, and it has been outstanding for five weeks.
- Transcript access from at least one partner (Granola/Gong/Fireflies export).
- gbrain substrate with the per-object scope field, before partner #2.
- Encryption at rest plus a context read/write audit log, before partner #2.
- T3 context-to-output instrumentation, from report #1 onward.

## The Assignment

**Get one Head of Product to grant read access to their Linear or Jira, their
roadmap or bets doc, and their last three customer call transcripts. Hand-build
one drift report. Send it Monday morning. Then watch whether they forward it.**

Do not demo, do not explain the brain, do not mention provenance tags, and do not
send a guided interview. Send the artifact.

Three things to capture on that first call, before any report exists:
1. How long their last exec update took to assemble, in hours.
2. One bet the team drifted off without anyone noticing, and what it cost.
3. Whether they will connect a transcript export — ask live, on that call. A no
   here weakens the whole spine and is better known in week one.

The report itself needs exactly three sections: what the documented bets are,
what actually shipped and got decided this week, and the three places those
disagree. Each finding cites its source. Nothing else.

**The forward is the metric.** A compliment is not a forward.
