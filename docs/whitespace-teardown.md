# Whitespace teardown: what a vertical AI OS actually looks like

**Source:** public marketing site at whitespacehq.ai, read 2026-07-29. Everything
below is from their own public copy. No private or non-public information.
**Why this company:** they sell an "AI operating system" to wholesale
distributors. Different industry, same structural problem: a vertical where the
domain knowledge lives in email, spreadsheets, and an aging system of record, and
where AI is useless until it has a model of that specific business.

Nobody studying "AI for PMs" looks at wholesale distribution. That is the point.

## The machine, in seven layers

| Layer | Whitespace | PM equivalent | Status here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wedge** | Inventory planning: forecast demand, recommend reorder quantity, flag dead stock. Weekly, denominated in cash | Weekly strategy drift report | chosen |
| **System of record** | Sits on top of the ERP. Their FAQ: "Do I need to replace my ERP? No." | Linear/Jira, Notion/Confluence, Gong/Granola, Slack | no migration required |
| **The brain** | "We build a living model of your business so you can set up AI agents for your unique processes." Never sold as memory | gbrain-class substrate, provenance-tagged | decided; must stop being the pitch |
| **The loop** | "You decide what it handles on its own and what gets routed to your team for review. Every correction trains the model, so accuracy compounds" | Head of Product marks "not drift, we changed that bet" → provenance-tagged decision written back | collapses the old Phase 4 into Phase 1 |
| **Expansion** | Inventory → sales/CS → AP/AR → weekly exec brief → what-if scenarios. Twelve use cases across four functions, eight sub-verticals named | Drift → exec readout → tradeoff modeling → ramp and handoff | earned by agent #2, not claimed by agent #1 |
| **Delivery** | Book a 30-minute call. Three steps: connect systems, set up agents, deploy with the team. Live in 2-4 weeks. Done-with-you | Same. The install is the consulting motion | legitimizes a capped services path |
| **Pricing** | No published price. Depends on usage volume and workflows. "Typically pays for itself within 3 months" | Payback as the anchor, not seat count | replaces $29-39/mo seats |

## What they never do

- **They never sell the brain.** The living model is mentioned once, as the reason
  agents work on your data. The headline numbers are £96k of cash freed, dead
  stock down 38%, stock turns at 6.2x, 80%+ productivity gains in key workflows,
  3-month payback. Nobody buys memory. People buy the thing memory enables.
- **They never claim the OS on day one.** The platform section opens with "Better
  inventory planning is just the start" — while listing twelve future use cases.
  They earned the OS framing by having one wedge live in production first.
- **They never promise omniscience.** The FAQ addresses "what if the AI makes a
  mistake" directly: calibrate before go-live, you choose what it handles alone,
  corrections train it. Trust is designed, not asserted.
- **They never offer self-serve.** No pricing page, no signup, no free tier. One
  Cal.com link.

## The layer that does not transfer

Four of the seven layers map onto a PM product cleanly. One breaks, and it is
load-bearing for the other six.

**Whitespace sells to someone with a P&L.** Their two named customers are a
co-founder and director, and an owner and managing director. A stock-out is cash.
A distributor already reorders weekly and already knows the reordering goes badly.

A PM individual contributor has no P&L, no budget, and no stock-out to point at.
This is why the "AI for PMs" category is stuck at $0-$39: free from Huryn, $49
once from Gupta, $15-29/mo from ChatPRD, $39/seat from mySecond. Every one of them
sells to the PM.

**The reframe:** a PM brain is not worth real money to a PM. It is worth real
money to whoever gets blamed when the team builds the wrong thing. Change the
buyer to Head of Product and the other six layers snap into place — there is a
weekly decision, a quantifiable cost, a system of record to sit on top of, and a
reason to pay at team rates instead of seat rates.

## What to copy, and what not to

**Copy:**
- The metric strip. Their "Stock weeks 8.9 / Availability 99.0% / Understocked 4
  SKUs / Reorder plan 18 POs" becomes "Bets on track 3 of 6 / Drift flagged 3 /
  Contradicted 1 / Sources read 51." An exec reads four numbers and knows whether
  to open the rest. This is what makes a report forwardable.
- Coverage honesty. Name what was *not* read. A drift report that implies it saw
  everything dies the first time it misses something.
- Correction as the training signal, not as a support ticket.
- Payback as the price anchor.
- One wedge live before the second agent ships.

**Do not copy:**
- The eight sub-verticals. They have customers in two and named eight. Naming a
  category you have not entered reads as ambition when you have a live wedge and
  as vapor when you do not.
- The productivity percentage. "80%+ productivity gains" is unfalsifiable and a
  Head of Product will discount it instantly. Points, weeks, and named drifted
  bets are checkable. Use those.
- Their two-to-four week install promise. That number is earned from repeated
  deployments. Do not put a timeline in writing before the first partner.

## The uncomfortable read

Whitespace is a founder with domain lineage (family in distribution for 30+ years,
plus BCG work advising manufacturers and distributors) selling into a vertical
where he has structural credibility. The PM equivalent of that advantage is real
here — the founder was a PM — but it is also the advantage every competitor in
the PM lane already has, and three of them have larger audiences.

The advantage that is not commoditized is the buyer choice. Nobody in the PM lane
is selling the drift loop to the person who owns the roadmap number.
