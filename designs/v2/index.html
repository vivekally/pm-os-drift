The problem

Nobody is checking the work against the plan, because nobody has time to read everything.

  • the strategy doc

    Accurate the week it was written. Stale within a month.and half of what you actually decided never made it in there

  • the decisions

    Made in a thread on a Tuesday afternoon, agreed by three people, never written down again.this is where most commitments live, and nothing reads it

  • the dashboards

    Report velocity and cycle time. Activity, not intent.they cannot tell you this sprint contradicts something you decided in June

  • the QBR

    "Why did we build that instead of this?" reconstructed from memory and search.hours of tab-opening, days before the meeting

How it works

One loop. Intent in, drift out, corrections back.

Most tools in this space ask you to maintain context. That is the thing nobody does. So this one builds the context from what your team is already doing, and asks you to correct it rather than write it.

  1. 1

    It reads where decisions happen

    Team chat, your delivery tracker, customer calls, and your documents if you have any. Read-only, always. A written strategy is optional, because most of what a team commits to was never written anywhere.

  2. 2

    It proposes what you have committed to

    Anything that reads like a commitment goes into a ledger as a proposal, tagged with where it came from and how sure we are. You confirm, correct, or dismiss each one. A wrong guess costs you one click and never comes back.

  3. 3

    Every Monday it reports where the work diverged

    Measured only against commitments you confirmed yourself, with every finding citing its sources. Nothing unconfirmed is ever used to tell you that you are off track, which is what makes a finding hard to argue with.

  4. 4

    Your corrections stick

    Reply "not drift, we changed that on purpose" and the commitment updates, dated, with your name on it. That finding never returns. The ledger gets more accurate every week you use it.

Why this is different

Everyone else sells you a memory. This tells you when you broke a promise.

What the category does

Answers "what do we know?"

Search, retrieval, notes, a company brain. All of it looks backwards at what exists, and none of it holds an opinion about what should have happened. That is a useful tool and a fair fight, and it is not this.

What this does

Answers "did we do what we said?"

It needs a view on what you committed to, which is why the ledger is the product and the report is the output. It is also why your corrections matter: they are the only thing that makes the view accurate, and they do not transfer to anyone else.

What it touches

Read-only, and specific about the edges.

What it readsThe channels you name · your delivery tracker · call transcripts you connect · documents you point it at
What it never doesWrite to your tools · post or comment as you · read direct messages · read channels you did not name
Where the ledger livesPlain text in a repository you control. Encryption at rest and an audit log of every read and write, before a second team is ever added.
What it admitsEvery report states what it did not read. A tool that implies total coverage gets caught being wrong once and is never opened again.
