01 The problem

The context tax is paid every session, by everyone, forever.

A PM opens Claude or ChatGPT and re-explains the company, the persona and the current bet before the first useful answer. The output is generic because the input was generic, it carries no sources, and the best prompt anyone wrote stays trapped in one browser history.

  • every session

    Ten minutes of context-setting before a useful answerthe AI guesses your product, invents a persona, returns a listicle

  • the current fix

    Static context files, which rot within weeksmySecond ships a "context health" nag because unmaintained context is the known failure mode

  • no receipts

    A PRD looks identical whether grounded or hallucinatedso everything gets re-verified by hand, erasing the time it saved

  • at team scale

    The same gap becomes strategy driftdocumented intent in Notion, actual behaviour in Linear and Slack, nobody reconciles them

03 The product

A memory with receipts, skills that cite it, and a loop that keeps it honest.

One guided sitting turns an interview plus your existing Notion exports, Jira boards and transcripts into 30 to 50 sourced claims. No cold start. The brain is plain markdown in your own repository, with no vector database and nothing pushed anywhere you don't control.

01 · Remember

Provenance on every claim

Each load-bearing fact is tagged documented, verbal, hunch or industry. Documented outweighs verbal outweighs intuition, in plain text so you can override it.

02 · Create

06 Who buys

Two entry points into the same product. Both held as hypotheses.

A · the individual

PM at 50–500p B2B SaaS, or a solo founder acting as their own PM

Already pays for AI tooling, adopts without procurement, feels the context tax every session. $29–39/mo, self-serve. Fastest path to users, slowest path to meaningful revenue.

B · the team

Head of Product or CPO running 5–15 PMs

Doesn't feel the context tax personally, but gets asked in a QBR why the team shipped X instead of Y. $200–500/mo team tier, or $500–2k done-with-you. Fewer buyers, far higher revenue each.

Working hypothesis: A acquires, B monetises, and the drift report is the bridge: the individual seeds a brain, the loop makes it visible to their boss, the boss buys for the team. Whether that bridge holds is the most important open question in this deck.

The honest constraint

That last assumption is the binding one. I start from zero audience against incumbents whose audiences compound daily, which is precisely why the team tier matters: five design-partner teams at $500/mo is the revenue of about sixty individual seats, sold into an empty channel instead of a crowded one.

My own drift, at small scale

A customer feature request was pulled into a sprint. The engineering team spent 50% of that sprint on technical debt instead. Nobody noticed until demo day, when the thing we had committed to a customer was not there. No document was wrong and nobody hid anything; the commitment and the work simply diverged, and no artifact anywhere compared the two. That delayed the customer signing their agreement by two months.

10 Where this actually is

Pre-revenue, pre-customer-conversation, and specific about it.

True today

The surface is built and public

68 skills, 42 workflows, a 13-screen prototype, a complete drift module with two weeks of reports and a setup flow, a landing page, and a fully cited competitive and market research pass. All solo, all live.

Not yet true

No customer conversations. None.

No PM has been interviewed, nobody has been asked to pay, and nobody outside me has used the prototype. In Forum's framework that is founder conviction, the weakest tier of demand evidence, and it is the gating weakness of this memo.

What that doesn't undermine: the problem. I lived it as a founding PM and again across three enterprise clients, and four funded or profitable companies sell adjacent solutions to the same person. What it does undermine is everything downstream: which entry point is right, what anyone pays, and whether provenance is a product or a feature people admire and don't buy.
